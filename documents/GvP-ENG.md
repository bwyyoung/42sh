# (ENGLISH) GROWTH VS PROFICIENCY

(FRENCH VERSION (TRANSLATED): https://forum.intra.42.fr/topics/16229/messages)

*Names have been changed to respect the identities of those involved*

Hello Friends,

I am writing this post in the forum, because I would like to share a recent experience I had in working at 42. This is regarding the Piscine C++, which took place from 9 to 22 January 2016.

During rush01 (21 - 22 January), I was assigned to work with a student named John Smith, and we were supposed to work together to finish ft_gkrellm.

**Please do not misunderstand me, as this post ISN’T strictly about him, and is NOT an attack AGAINST him, but rather about 42 as a whole, and the meaning of the work we do here.**

## RUSH01

On Friday night of the rush (20 January), I messaged him and asked whether we could start working that night, instead of Saturday morning. This is because the rush is due Sunday night, at 11:42pm. I told him that I would be at E3.

He replied to me and said he could not make it on Friday night, as he was working during the weekend. He said he would show up on Saturday early morning to start work. In the meantime, I begin preliminary work on rush01, and planned to introduce him to the progress I had made, when he arrived the next day.

On Saturday morning, I continued working on the project and made as much progress as I could. I soon became worried, and messaged John Smith again, asking where he was.

**He replied:**

*“Oh Sorry! I meant to say that I was working during the weekend on Saturday, and would only show up on Sunday. Just Git Push all of your work, and I will take over on Sunday.”*

Understandably, I was furious, and called him several times.
There was no answer.

At this point, I knew that John Smith was selfish and unreliable, and could not count on him to finish the project. There were 2 options:

1. Join another team and help them with their project (Forming a 3 player team). Scrap whatever work I had already done.
2. Work alone and solo the project, in order to continue on the progress I had already made.

In the end, I worked 48 hours alone NON-STOP during the weekend in order to finish the rush. I was at E3 during the entire time, and worked throughout Saturday night and Sunday morning. If I were to join another team, I feared that it would lead to a case of “too many chefs in the kitchen”.

During that period, I did not see John Smith, and he did not contact me at all on Sunday, during the rush, after his last message. If he wanted to find me, he would have found me at E3, as there were only 5 people on that floor on Sunday morning. If he wanted to work, but could not make it to school, he would have at least downloaded the work I pushed on GIT, and asked me questions through slack, sms, phone call, or email. None of that occurred, which is why I had to work the 48 hours as I had no time to chase him, and had to assume the worst.

## RESULTS

https://projects.intra.42.fr/piscine-cpp-rush01/byoung-w

After the rush, I received no contact from John Smith, and knew that he had abandoned the project. I then booked for corrections for rush01, and explained to each of my correctors the situation I was in. John Smith was not present for the corrections, at the time. They saw the project, and gave my project’s team 125 marks. I was extremely happy and satisfied to see my work recognised for excellence. However, I felt that I needed to report to the bocal, that John Smith did not do any work during this project, and that he should not be awarded the same marks as me.

## THE BOCAL
I emailed the bocal within 2 days after the piscine had ended, and wrote to them about my rush01. After 1 week of waiting, I did not get an answer from any staff member. I then approached Franck from the Bocal, and asked for help.

He told me that Thor would be back the following week, and only he could address the issues with Piscine C++, as he was the one who authored it. Soon, I sat down and spoke to Thor, and explained the entire situation to him.

**His answer:**

*Can I take away marks from your teammate? YES, I can.*

*Do I WANT to do it? I don’t know.*

Thor explained to me that how teams are managed are decided by the students, and should only be managed by students, and NOT the bocal. If he were to be a judge, and say “You get 0 marks, you get full marks, etc..”, it would make Ecole 42 a primary school with politics, and NOT the school that it is today. He also stated that in the real world, there will be colleagues who get rewarded, but do not deserve it. Therefore, as 42 is trying to simulate the real world, he does not want to intervene in the points awarded to individuals in a group. As for how I should deal with John Smith, he told me that I should warn other students about my experience with him, and advise them accordingly. This is part of the reason why I am writing this post.

## CONCLUSION

I accepted Thor’s answer, and hold no personal grudge against John Smith, but still have questions that involve the long term repercussions of such events at 42. Although our school is unique, and has both positives and negatives, some of its objectives are still unclear, and philosophies under question. I personally have always felt that there was a dissonance between how we have been conditioned, and what we have been expected to deliver.

**Why do students, like the teammate I encountered, exist in 42, when 42 is known to be among the best schools in France(so far), and has the most avant-garde pedagogy of all Computer Science schools, in recent years?**

**Is it necessarily their fault, that they behave this way?**

Based on feedback from Thor, I understand that 42 values student GROWTH(how much students improve), instead of student PROFICIENCY(how many students meet a certain score to be deemed proficient). This means that we value how much a student evolves over time, rather than how many marks he can achieve in an exam. This is because, If 42 were to value proficiency instead of growth, the real world consideration of politics and self-reliance/self-management would not have been taken into account.

42 is right to value growth, as that is the proper metric to determine the quality of a school.*

Having said that, we have an intra system that measures a student’s progress by level, and this level is determined by the marks earned during projects (proficiency). As a result, students tend to end up comparing themselves to one another using LEVELS as a metric, rather than growth, or proven raw skills. Finishing projects quickly, one after another, doesn't necessarily mean that the student had reasonable growth. Therefore, there is a conflict in messaging towards the student body on how one can BEST GROW in 42.

For example, I have observed that some students enjoy playing video games in their free time, much more than furthering their education useful skills/knowledge for the future (Not necessarily 42 related work, but educating/improving themselves in general). This is because in a game designed like Street Fighter V/League of Legends, a sense of GROWTH is more immediately apparent and satisfying when learning to master the game, and finish it. Games are designed with immediate feedback cues that highlight growth and mastery, and that you do not necessarily need to be proficient in order to enjoy them.

## QUESTIONS

This leaves me with 3 questions I would like to ask to you, the 42 community:

1. Is the Intra a reliable way to measure the skill level of a student, and how strong they are not just as coders, but as engineers? I personally feel that the answer is no, because given that students can take advantage of other’s work by having a similar situation to mine, they gain levels and marks that they do not necessarily deserve. Also, some students can transform solo projects into group based projects, and divide work among themselves in order to finish faster, but not necessarily learn (GROW) as much.
    1. In that case, HOW do we measure the true competence of a student at 42, given that a level is just a number, and not in any way representative of true skill?
    2. Is there a way to measure GROWTH, rather than PROFICIENCY in 42?
2. If students can fake their way through 42, or simply avoid doing work when it is necessary, what are the ways we can eliminate this behaviour, and convince them to change? I feel that we as a community need to enforce a culture of excellence in 42, and help others drive performance through hard work and perseverance.
    1. This is because if this behaviour is not addressed, and if students simply do not care about their place in 42, the behaviour becomes cancerous, infectious, and can spread to more impressionable students.
    2. Or in some cases, students who wish to excel in 42 may end up avoiding groupwork, in order to avoid meeting “phantom” students, or simply give up 42 because of their experiences, should it be overwhelmingly negative.
    3. Frankly, such individuals in their current state, are a wasted space in 42, and someone who worked extremely hard in the Piscine C to qualify for 42, but did not get in, may deserve to study here more than these folks. This is because the unqualified student may have valued his growth significantly, but it was not observed from the outside.
3. Are there better ways to benchmark students against real world industry requirements and competences?
    1. Although 42 has done a fantastic job in teaching students to work together in peer-to-peer learning, a school in itself is still a virtual environment that does not represent real industry preparedness.
    2. Internships DO NOT count, as they would normally provide limited feedback in helping a student understand his/her true standing compared to industry veterans. Also, workloads are generally more lightweight and are given to interns with reduced risk/losses to the company, should the intern fail.
    3. This means that students can be lulled into a false sense of security regarding their perceived skill level (how good they think they are) versus real world standings (how good the industry thinks they are), and become complacent.
    4. For example, friends and family could say that you are smart, and a good engineer. During work in school projects, you may believe that you are a good engineer. But the real world says otherwise.
    5. *It is not about what you know; it’s about what you can prove.*

## SOLUTIONS

I have suggested several solutions that might help promote growth, and better measure it in 42.

**What do you think? Would you have any ideas for other solutions?**

1. Most Improved Student Awards
    1. Give stars and 1UPs to students who demonstrate real world improvement, and highlight it in the intra. The awards can be given by both students and staff
    2. Have a leaderboard showcasing students who have the most improvement by ranking. Therefore, highlight growth.
    3. Encourage students to grow daily and keep focused.
2. Student to Student Appraisal on Profile Page
    1. Genuine feedback between students regarding metrics that are harder to measure. This includes activity, participation, leadership, teamwork and overall pleasantness to work with.
    2. Tell students the areas they are weak in, and need to improve further in order to grow.
    3. Similar to LinkedIn Feedback and Appraisal
3. Showcase and highlight unique projects developed under 42 Cursus
    1. Allow upload of screenshots of projects, as well as writing a project summary describing the project, and what makes it unique
    2. These projects are shown in a “News Feed” like format, and highlight new and interesting projects.
    3. Projects can be starred/1UP’ed if students find them special, or simply cool.
    4. If a student wants to learn more about the project, he can contact the author to sit down and discuss how it was made, and LEARN from it.
4. More to be added...

I admit that I could be wrong in my analysis and proposal, but still would like to share it with 42.
